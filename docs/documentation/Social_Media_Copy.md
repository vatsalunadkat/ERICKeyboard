## Focus and voice

Keep the language very simple. Short words, short sentences, plain meaning. Every post should pull from these 12 things (most important first):

1. For users with disability
2. For one-handed users
3. Ad free
4. Forever free, no hidden costs
5. Privacy focused (low priority)
6. Gentle, calm, decluttered
7. For autistic users — a clear, logical A to Z layout
8. Colorblind color options and dyslexia-friendly fonts
9. Fully customizable
10. Backed by research
11. Support for all types of controllers
12. Built for motor needs, pain, fatigue, and one-handed use

Research note for public posts:
- Safe claim: related chorded-keyboard research in this repo supports lower movement, strong motor-accessibility potential, and speed gains with practice.
- Unsafe claim: do not say research already proves autistic users type faster with ERICK than with QWERTY. The repo research summary says that autism-specific speed claim is not directly established.

## LinkedIn

ERICK is a keyboard for people who find normal phone keyboards hard to use.

Instead of lots of tiny keys, you type with two big dials. Less reach. Bigger targets. Fewer mistakes.

It is built for disabled users first — people with motor needs, pain, fatigue, or the use of one hand. The layout is a clear A to Z order, which can help autistic users and anyone who wants typing to be easy to predict.

What makes it different:
- For users with disability, motor needs, pain and fatigue
- Type with one hand, or with any game controller (Xbox, PlayStation, 8BitDo and more)
- Ad free, and free forever with no hidden costs
- Calm and clear, with no clutter
- Colorblind-safe colors and dyslexia-friendly fonts
- Fully customizable colors, layouts and fonts
- Your typing stays on your phone
- Built on accessibility research

The research here is strongest for motor accessibility and lower movement.

## Reddit

Title: I made a keyboard for people who struggle with tiny phone keys

Body:
ERICK is a keyboard built for disabled users first.

Instead of tapping lots of tiny keys, you type with two big dials. The goal is less movement, bigger targets, and a calm, clear screen.

It is made for people with motor needs, pain, fatigue, or one-handed use. The layout is a simple A to Z order, which can help autistic users and anyone who wants something easy to predict. There are colorblind-safe colors and a dyslexia-friendly font too. You can also type with any game controller instead of touch.

It is ad free and free forever, with no hidden costs. Your typing stays on your phone.

On research: the work here is strongest for motor accessibility and lower movement. I am not claiming research proves autistic users type faster than on QWERTY — that still needs direct studies. What I can say is that the layout is clearer and easier to predict, and related chord-keyboard studies show good long-term speed with practice.
